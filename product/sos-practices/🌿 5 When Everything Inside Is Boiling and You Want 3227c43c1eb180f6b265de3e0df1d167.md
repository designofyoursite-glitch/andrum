## Context

**State Description:**

Something is burning inside you — a pull toward the phone, the urge to explain, to fix, to make sure everything is still okay. Adrenaline is up. It feels urgent. The body has somewhere it wants to go, and the mind is still deciding whether sending that message is the right direction.

**Key Goal:**

To release excess energy through movement, reduce the intensity of the impulse, discharge tension from the body, and return the ability to choose — rather than react.

**When to Use:**

- When your hand is reaching for the phone
- Best used with headphones during a 5–10 minute walk outside
- An ideal emergency practice before sending a message you might later regret

---

## Breathing Technique: Rhythmic Walking Breath

**Inhale 4 steps → Exhale 6 steps**

**How:**

- Inhale through the nose for 4 steps
- Exhale slowly for 6 steps (through the mouth or nose)
- Keep it natural — if your stride changes, let the breath adjust with you

**Why it works:**

- Movement metabolises adrenaline — the body needs somewhere to go with this energy
- The rhythmic pattern creates a "doing something" that redirects the impulse to act
- The longer exhale gradually activates the parasympathetic nervous system, signalling: *you are not in danger. You can slow down.*
- Together, breath and movement create a powerful discharge without escalating

## 5-Minute Guided Meditation Script

*"You don't have to hold still to find stillness. Sometimes you find it one step at a time."*

### Introduction & Finding Your Ground (30 seconds)

Something is pulling hard right now — that urge to reach out, to fix it, to know.

We're not going to fight it. We're going to walk it out, I’ve got you. 

Earbuds in. Start moving. Let's walk our way to some clarity.

*Notice your feet. The ground is already holding you — one step at a time.*

Feel how your weight shifts from one foot to another 

Pause

---

### Rhythmic Walking Breath (3–4 minutes)

Now let's bring the breath into the movement. 

We're going to inhale for **4 steps**, and exhale for **6 steps.**

Every exhale is a message to your body: *we are not in danger. We can slow down.* 

*If it feels right, let the exhale make a sound — even just a quiet sigh through your mouth. (example breath sound)*

Let's find the rhythm.

*Inhale... 2... 3... 4*

*Exhale... 2... 3... 4... 5... 6*

*Inhale... 2... 3... 4*

*Exhale... 2... 3... 4... 5... 6*

*Inhale... 2... 3... 4*

*Exhale... 2... 3... 4... 5... 6*

*Inhale... 2... 3... 4*

*Exhale... 2... 3... 4... 5... 6*

**Stay with the rhythm. Let your body do the work.**

*(Quieter)*

Inhale (sound of breath for 4 steps)

Exhale (sound of breath for 6 steps)

If the impulse flares up again — that's okay. Don't fight it. Just exhale a little longer.

*Inhale... 2... 3... 4*

*Exhale... 2... 3... 4... 5... 6*

*Pause* *30 seconds — continue rhythm (sound of breath in background)*

### Affirmations (1 minute) *(sound of breath softly in background)*

As you keep walking and breathing, let these ride alongside the rhythm.

One word. One breath. One step at a time.

*Pause between each*

*Pause*

**"You're walking."**

*Exhale... (silent count of 6 steps)*

---

### Self-Energy Check-In (30 seconds)

Let the breath return to its natural rhythm now.

*Pause*

Notice what has shifted — even slightly. Has the urgency softened at all? Has the body found a little more space?

*Pause*

Can you feel any curiosity about what's underneath the impulse — rather than just the pull to act on it?

*Pause*

If yes, let that be enough. If not — that's okay too. You moved your body. You stayed with yourself. That matters more than you know.

---

### Closing (30 seconds)

The urge to reach out isn't wrong.

It just needed somewhere to go first.

And you gave it somewhere to go.

Before you head back — take one last slow breath with me.

*Pause*

And let it go.

*Pause*

Whenever you're ready, gently bring your attention back.

You walked yourself home.
