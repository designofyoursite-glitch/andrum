# 🌿 7. When Anger Rises and You Want to Tell Everyone Off (1)

<aside>
🌿

**Guided Meditation Script | 5–10 Minutes**

*For: anger / irritation / resentment / the urge to lash out*

</aside>

---

*"Anger is not the enemy. Anger is the alarm. What you do with it — that's where the choice lives."*

---

## Context

**State Description:**

Irritation, sharpness, resentment, or rage are boiling up. There's an urge to lash out, snap, or say something that might feel satisfying for about ten seconds. The body is tense. The inner voice is loud and not very kind.

**Key Goal:**

To give anger a safe, physical space to move through the body — without it moving into your relationships. And to return to clarity and the ability to choose your next move.

**When to Use:**

- When you feel emotionally overheated
- Before sending a harsh message or saying something sharp
- Can also be practiced regularly to build the capacity to feel anger without being consumed by it

---

## Breathing Technique: Lion's Breath → 4–6 Stabilisation

### Lion's Breath (6 rounds)

**How:**

- Take a deep inhale through the nose
- Open the mouth wide, stick out the tongue, and exhale forcefully with a strong "haaa"
- This is supposed to feel intense and physical — that's the point

### 4–6 Stabilisation

**How:**

- Inhale through the nose for 4
- Exhale slowly for 6

**Why it works:**

- Anger is activation energy — it needs an outlet, not suppression. Lion's Breath gives it a sanctioned, physical exit.
- The forceful exhale discharges tension from the face, throat, and chest — the places anger tends to live
- The transition to 4–6 brings the nervous system into regulation once the peak has passed

<aside>
🧡

**Note:** Lion's Breath is meant to feel loud and a little wild before it feels calm. If it feels intense — you're doing it right. The settling comes after.

</aside>

## Guided Meditation Script

*"Anger is not the enemy. Anger is the alarm. What you do with it — that's where the choice lives."*

### Introduction (30 seconds)

*You're feeling it — the irritation, the anger, maybe even the fury.*

The heat is rising and you want to explode

That heat is real. Let's give it somewhere to go.

Make sure your in a place you feel comfortable to make sound.

---

### Locating the Anger (45 seconds)

Before anything else — let's find where the anger is sitting in your body.

Your jaw? Your chest? Your hands? Your throat?

Just try locate it. We don’t need to change it.

---

### Lion's Breath (45 seconds–1 minute)

We're going to do some rounds of Lion's Breath

*This breath is meant to be felt and heard. Let it be as big as it needs to be*

Take a deep inhale through the nose...

Then open your mouth wide, stick your tongue out, and exhale with a strong **"haaa."**

Let it out. All of it. Don't be polite about it.

Ready?

*Inhale...*

*And exhale — open, tongue out — HAAA. (sound multiple people breathing)*

Again.

*Inhale...*

*HAAA.*

*Inhale...*

*HAAA.*

*Inhale...*

*HAAA.*

*Inhale...*

*HAAA.*

One more.

*Inhale...*

*And let it go.*

*(It's meant to feel intense before it feels calm. Keep going.)*

---

### 4–6 Stabilisation (2–3 minutes)

Now we shift.

Soften your face. Let your jaw settle. Try to relax your chest and your belly. 

We're going to move into 4-6 breathing— **inhale through the nose for 4, exhale through the nose or mouth for 6.**

If you feel comfortable close down your eyes. Lets begin.

*Inhale… 2, 3, 4*

*Exhale… 2, 3, 4, 5, 6*

*Inhale… 2, 3, 4*

*Exhale… 2, 3, 4, 5, 6*

*Inhale… 2, 3, 4*

*Exhale… 2, 3, 4, 5, 6*

If the heat rises again- *Feel free to bring the sound back on the exhale if you need it*

*Inhale… 2, 3, 4*

*Exhale… 2, 3, 4, 5, 6*

*Inhale… 2, 3, 4*

*Exhale… 2, 3, 4, 5, 6*

*Inhale… 2, 3, 4*

*Exhale… 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (have sound on exhale)*

*Pause* *30 seconds — continue rhythm (sound of breath in background)*

---

### Self-Energy Check-In (30 seconds)

Let the breath return to its natural rhythm now.

*Pause*

Just notice — has anything shifted? Even a little?

*Pause*

---

### Closing (30 seconds)

Allowing anger to move through the body without reacting is a skill

You just practiced something most people never learn.

This feeling came to protect something in you. See if you can let it be here, maybe even welcome it or thank it — and then choose what happens next from here.

Let's take one last breath together.

*Pause*

And let it go.

*Pause*

Whenever you're ready, open your eyes.

Welcome back
