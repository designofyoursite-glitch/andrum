# Language Pattern Guide

Use this guide when writing Andrum Reels, captions, carousels, and hooks from audience language.

The goal is not to sound therapeutic. The goal is to sound emotionally precise.

## Core Voice

Andrum language should feel like:

- "Oh. That is exactly what this feels like."
- "I have never heard it said that clearly."
- "This is specific enough that I trust it."

It should not feel like:

- A lecture.
- A diagnosis.
- A workshop script.
- A psychology explainer.

## The Andrum Sentence Shape

Use this structure often:

> Small real-life moment + body reaction + emotional meaning + gentle reframe.

Example:

> They do not reply for a few hours, and suddenly your chest is tight, your phone feels impossible to put down, and your body is acting like silence means danger.

## High-Value Word Banks

### Real-life triggers

- no reply
- left on read
- shorter message
- different tone
- they were online
- two weeks of silence
- "busy" without warmth
- warm in person, distant over text
- low-effort message
- talking about their ex
- not defining the relationship
- acting like a couple without choosing you

### Body sensations

- chest tightens
- stomach drops
- body buzzes
- heart races
- breath gets shallow
- hands shake
- everything inside tightens
- trapped in your own body
- frozen
- urgent
- heavy
- restless
- wired

### Internal actions

- replaying
- checking
- rereading
- drafting and deleting
- scanning
- comparing
- reading between the lines
- making a case against yourself
- trying to act normal
- panic privately
- waiting for proof

### Emotional meanings

- "I am losing them."
- "I did something wrong."
- "I am too much."
- "I am not enough."
- "They do not care."
- "I have to fix this now."
- "If I stop trying, it disappears."
- "The real me is too hard to love."

## Strong Language Structures

### "It was just..., but..."

Use when the outside event looks small and the inner reaction is huge.

Examples:

- It was just one unanswered text, but your body read it as the connection disappearing.
- It was just a colder message, but something in you started preparing to be left.

### "You are not..., you are..."

Use for shame relief and reframes.

Examples:

- You are not being dramatic. Your body is trying to get back to safety.
- You are not too much. You are asking for steadiness from someone inconsistent.

### "The worst part is not..."

Use to go deeper than the obvious pain.

Examples:

- The worst part is not that they did not reply. It is how fast you turned it into proof that you are hard to love.
- The worst part is not missing them. It is needing comfort from the person who made you panic.

### "Your mind knows..., but your body..."

Use for breakup, letting go, and nervous system lag.

Examples:

- Your mind knows they were inconsistent. Your body still wants the message that makes it stop hurting.
- Your mind knows it is over. Your body is still waiting for the old relief.

### "They can..., and still..."

Use for contradiction and emotional maturity.

Examples:

- They can care about you and still not choose you clearly.
- They can miss you and still not be able to show up.

### "You do not miss..., you miss..."

Use for attachment clarity.

Examples:

- You do not always miss the person. Sometimes you miss the relief their attention gave your body.
- You do not miss the chaos. You miss the tiny moment when it finally stopped.

## Phrases To Avoid

- "Regulate your nervous system" unless translated into a felt moment.
- "Heal your attachment wounds" unless made concrete.
- "Meet your needs" when "ask for steadiness" or "want reassurance" is more human.
- "Communicate authentically" when "say the honest thing without making it a rescue mission" is sharper.
- "Hold space."
- "Somatic awareness."
- "Secure attachment journey."
- "Inner child work" unless the post is intentionally about that.

## Rewrite Rule

If a sentence sounds like psychology, ask:

> What would this look like at 1:17 a.m. with the phone in her hand?

Then write that.
