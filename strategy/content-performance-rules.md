# Content Performance Rules

Use this as the quality bar for Andrum Instagram content.

The content should not feel like education. It should feel like someone naming the exact thing happening inside the reader.

## Do Not Drift Into

- Generic validation content.
- Therapy lecture tone.
- Motivational coaching.
- Abstract emotional language.
- Overexplaining nervous system concepts.
- Polished inspirational language.
- "Healing journey" tone.
- Therapist phrasing.
- Spiritual cliches.
- Generic self-love advice.

## The Content Must Feel

- Instantly understandable.
- Emotionally recognizable.
- Easy to consume.
- Emotionally safe.
- Highly relatable.
- Internet-native.

## Prioritize

- Fast emotional recognition.
- Specific behaviors.
- Short emotional hooks.
- Contradiction.
- Shame recognition.
- Emotionally exposed tone.
- Anxious attachment spirals.
- Messy inner monologues.
- Self-aware irrational thoughts.
- Emotional overanalysis.
- Body reactions to relationship triggers.

## Audience Reaction Goal

The audience should feel:

> "This is exactly what happens inside me."

Not:

> "This is educational content."

Optimize for comments like:

> "holy shit this is me"

## Primary Content Priority

The primary goal is not education.

The primary goal is:

> emotional recognition and relatability.

If forced to choose between psychological precision and emotional recognition, always prioritize emotional recognition.

This does not mean being sloppy or false. It means translating the idea into the version the audience can feel instantly.

## Writing Rhythm

Prefer:

- Short lines.
- Fast comprehension.
- Conversational rhythm.
- Emotionally charged phrasing.
- Internet-native structure.

Avoid:

- Long setup.
- Slow explanations.
- Essay structure.
- Dense paragraphs.
- Formal transitions.

## Emotional Realism

The content should feel emotionally exposed, not emotionally polished.

Prefer:

- Awkward honesty.
- Contradiction.
- Irrational emotional logic.
- Self-awareness.
- Messy inner experiences.

Avoid:

- Resolved wisdom.
- Emotionally perfect conclusions.
- Clean healing narratives.

Strong:

> I know checking will make me feel worse, but not checking feels physically impossible.

Weak:

> I am learning to honor myself and choose peace.

## Body-First Filter

Whenever possible, translate emotional states into body experiences.

Use:

- Chest tightness.
- Urge to check the phone.
- Stomach drop.
- Adrenaline spike.
- Numbness.
- Relief waves.
- Dissociation.
- Shaking.
- Hyperfocus.
- Shallow breath.
- Frozen body.
- Hands reaching for the phone before the mind catches up.

Do not stay only in abstract thoughts.

Weak:

> I feel anxious about the relationship.

Strong:

> My stomach drops before I even open the message.

## Anti-AI Rules

Avoid:

- Symmetrical sentence structure.
- Generic emotional summaries.
- Repetitive validation phrases.
- Obvious AI phrasing.
- Content that sounds optimized but emotionally empty.

The writing should feel:

- Specific.
- Human.
- Slightly imperfect.
- Emotionally lived-in.

If a line sounds too smooth, add one real detail:

- A behavior.
- A body sensation.
- A contradiction.
- A private thought.
- An embarrassing impulse.

## Format Differentiation

### Reels

Reels should create an immediate emotional hit.

Use:

- Short hook-first writing.
- Low cognitive load.
- Emotional recognition first.
- One clear feeling or contradiction.
- Minimal explanation.

### Carousels

Carousels can go slightly deeper than Reels.

Use:

- Clear structure.
- Simple body or nervous system insight.
- Swipe momentum.
- One idea per slide.
- Emotional recognition before explanation.

## High-Performing Patterns

### Contradiction

Use when two truths are happening at once.

Examples:

- Wanting reassurance but distrusting it once it arrives.
- Missing someone but panicking when they text.
- Knowing something is irrational while still reacting physically.
- Preparing for abandonment before anything happens.

### Internal Conflict

Show the fight between the calm thought and the anxious body.

Example:

> I know they are probably busy, but my body is already acting like I have been left.

### Emotionally Irrational Logic

Name the logic that only makes sense inside the spiral.

Example:

> If I send one perfect message, maybe I can stop this from falling apart.

### Repetitive Thoughts

Use loops, not conclusions.

Example:

> Maybe they are busy. Maybe they are annoyed. Maybe I said too much. Maybe this is the beginning of the end.

### Hypervigilance

Show the checking, scanning, comparing, and reading into tiny changes.

Example:

> You are not just reading the message. You are reading the time, the tone, the punctuation, and the version of them you got yesterday.

### Panic Before Certainty

This is a core Andrum pattern.

Example:

> Nothing has happened yet, but your body is already preparing for the worst version.

### Nervous System Anticipation

Keep it simple and body-based.

Example:

> Your chest tightens before there is proof.

### Emotionally Embarrassing Behaviors

These create strong recognition.

Examples:

- Drafting and deleting the same message.
- Checking when they were last online.
- Rereading the conversation for hidden signs.
- Pretending not to care while watching the phone.
- Feeling calmer only after they reply.
- Getting the reply and still not trusting it.

## Strong Emotional Structures

### Wanting Reassurance But Distrusting It Once It Arrives

Hook:

> The reply finally comes, and somehow your body still does not relax.

Why it works:

It captures the part people feel ashamed to admit: reassurance can help for five minutes and then the doubt comes back.

### Missing Someone But Panicking When They Text

Hook:

> You wanted them to text. Then they did, and your whole body froze.

Why it works:

It shows that longing and fear can live in the same moment.

### Knowing It Is Irrational While Still Reacting Physically

Hook:

> You know it is probably nothing. Your stomach drops anyway.

Why it works:

It respects the reader's intelligence while naming the body reaction.

### Preparing For Abandonment Before Anything Happens

Hook:

> They have not left. Your body is already rehearsing it.

Why it works:

It names panic before certainty, one of the most relatable anxious attachment experiences.

## Retention Rules

- Start inside the moment.
- Make the first line specific.
- Use short sentences.
- Show the spiral unfolding.
- Add a contradiction by line two or three.
- Avoid explaining the whole concept.
- Give one emotional turn, not a full lesson.
- End with recognition, a tiny reframe, or a body-based pause.

## Save And Share Rules

People save content when it gives language to something they could not explain.

People share content when it feels like:

- "This is me."
- "This is us."
- "This explains why I act like this."
- "I need my friend to see this."
- "I wish someone had said this to me earlier."

## Avoid Long Paragraphs

For Reels and carousels, keep copy tight.

Weak:

> When someone you care about does not respond, it can activate old relational wounds and create nervous system dysregulation that makes you feel unsafe.

Strong:

> They do not reply, and your body starts acting like the connection is gone.

## Final Quality Check

Before publishing, ask:

- Is this instantly understandable?
- Is there a specific behavior?
- Is there a private thought?
- Is there a contradiction?
- Is there a body reaction?
- Does it avoid sounding educational?
- Would someone comment "holy shit this is me"?
- Can it be consumed in a few seconds?
