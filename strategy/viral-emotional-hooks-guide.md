# Viral Emotional Hooks Guide

Use this guide to create hooks that feel emotionally specific, internet-native, and immediately recognizable.

## What Makes A Hook Work

A strong Andrum hook usually does one of four things:

- Names a private moment people rarely say out loud.
- Makes a tiny trigger feel emotionally visible.
- Turns shame into recognition.
- Explains the contradiction without sounding clinical.

## Hook Categories

### 1. No Reply Hooks

- When they do not reply and your body starts writing the breakup for them.
- The text is unanswered, but your nervous system has already built a whole story.
- If a delayed reply makes your chest tighten, read this.
- That moment when silence starts feeling like evidence.
- You are not waiting for a text. You are waiting for your body to feel safe again.

### 2. Too Much / Not Enough Hooks

- You are not too much. You are just tired of being met halfway.
- The shame after asking for reassurance is sometimes worse than the anxiety itself.
- If you keep shrinking so they stay, this is for you.
- You are not hard to love because you need consistency.
- Maybe you are not too intense. Maybe the connection is too unstable.

### 3. Situationship Hooks

- It feels like a relationship when you are together and like nothing when you leave.
- They act close enough to keep you attached, but not clear enough to let you relax.
- The most confusing people give warmth without stability.
- If they say they want nothing serious but treat you like a partner, your body will get confused.
- Mixed signals are not romantic when your nervous system is paying the price.

### 4. Pain As Closeness Hooks

- Sometimes you do not miss them. You miss the relief after the panic.
- The person who triggers the anxiety can start feeling like the only person who can calm it.
- Pain can feel like closeness when calm love still feels unfamiliar.
- If chaos feels more magnetic than peace, there is a reason.
- You are not addicted to them. You may be addicted to the relief cycle.

### 5. Body Reaction Hooks

- Your body reacts before your brain has time to be reasonable.
- The stomach drop is real, even when the trigger looks small.
- You cannot logic your way out of a body alarm.
- When everything inside tightens, the first step is not another message.
- Your chest knows you are scared before your mind finds the story.

### 6. Self-Abandonment Hooks

- The moment you start becoming easier to keep someone, pause.
- If loving them means leaving yourself, your body will eventually protest.
- You do not have to become low-maintenance to be chosen.
- The crumb feels powerful when you have forgotten you are allowed to want the meal.
- Stop making yourself smaller for someone who still cannot meet you.

### 7. Letting Go Hooks

- Your mind accepted it. Your body is still waiting.
- Letting go is hard when the pain still has their name on it.
- Sometimes closure is not a conversation. It is your body realizing the danger has passed.
- You can miss them and still choose not to reopen the wound.
- The urge to go back is not always love. Sometimes it is withdrawal from the familiar.

## Hook Formulas

### Tiny trigger, huge body response

> When [small trigger] and your [body sensation] before you can think.

Example:

> When they reply colder than usual and your stomach drops before you can think.

### Shame relief

> You are not [shame label]. You are [more accurate human truth].

Example:

> You are not dramatic. Your body is trying to feel chosen again.

### Contradiction

> They [warm behavior], but [unstable reality].

Example:

> They held you like a partner, but left you guessing like an option.

### Hidden cost

> The hardest part is not [obvious pain]. It is [deeper private pain].

Example:

> The hardest part is not that they pulled away. It is how quickly you blamed yourself for it.

### Nervous system translation

> Your body is not reacting to [surface event]. It is reacting to [emotional meaning].

Example:

> Your body is not reacting to one text. It is reacting to the possibility of being left.

## Hook Quality Checklist

- Does it name a specific moment?
- Can someone picture the phone, room, or conversation?
- Does it include a body feeling or private thought?
- Does it avoid sounding like a diagnosis?
- Would someone send it to a friend with "this is me"?
- Does the post actually deliver relief, clarity, or a next step?
