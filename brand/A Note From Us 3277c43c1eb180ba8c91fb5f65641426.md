# A Note From Us

**Hey love,**

If you’re reading this, there’s a good chance you’re in that familiar place — the *“why am I like this?”* place.

Maybe you saw they were online but didn’t reply. Maybe the conversation ended a little colder than it started. And suddenly your whole body is doing its thing before your mind has a chance to catch up: chest tight, stomach dropped, hands shaking while you type and delete, the urge to check one more time — and then one more time again.

We want to say this clearly, right at the beginning: **you are not crazy.** You are not “too much,” and you are not broken. What’s happening is real, and it’s physical. When someone pulls away (or even *might* be pulling away), your nervous system can treat uncertainty like danger — which is why “just meditate” can feel impossible, journaling can feel too slow, and therapy can be amazing on a Tuesday afternoon but still not reach you at 2am when your body is in full hijack mode.

We know this because we’ve lived it. We’ve been the person on the bathroom floor. We’ve been the person replaying the last conversation a hundred times. We’ve been the person who wanted one small scrap of reassurance so badly it hurt — and then felt ashamed for wanting it. And we learned something that changed everything for us: Coming back to yourself doesn’t start with the perfect thought. It starts with the body.

That’s why we created Andrum, and why we made the SOS Practices the way we did — not soft, not robotic, not “zen,” but short, audio-first practices you can press when the wave hits.

Think of them as a circuit-breaker: something that helps your body come down enough for you to find the pause again. Not so you can become someone else. Not so you can become “chill.” Just so you can come back to **ground** — and make the next choice from you, not from panic.

If you’ve been living inside the spiral, we want you to hear this: there is nothing wrong with the part of you that reaches for closeness, there is nothing shameful about needing reassurance, and you do not have to fight your nervous system alone.

So when you’re ready, press play. Let the voice hold the count, and let your body get the message it’s been begging for: *you’re safe enough to breathe right now.* And if, after the practice, you still want to reach out, you’ll do it from a steadier place — the place where you can speak with honesty instead of urgency.

We’re with you. Breathe.

*With love,*

Ksusha, Masha, Bec
