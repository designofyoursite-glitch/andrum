## 01 · Core concept

### The name: Andrum

Andrum is a Swedish word meaning *“space to breath.”* It is not only a physical metaphor. It is a precise description of what the practice creates: **a space where an automatic reaction used to be.**

> ◆ The space between inhale and exhale — a physiological pause
◆ The space between trigger and reaction — a psychological pause
◆ The space of inner dignity — the place from which new choices are made
> 

### Anchor · Allow · Align — the three levels

This is not just a product description. It is the path a woman walks every time she uses a practice.

| **LEVEL 1**
**ANCHOR**
*Come back to the body*

A trigger fires a physiological response: racing heart, heat, tightness, freeze. Breath works at the level of the nervous system: it lowers heart rate, activates the vagus nerve, returns the body to safety. **This is the entry point.** Nothing else is possible without this first step. | **LEVEL 2**
**ALLOW**
*Open the space*

When the body has settled, a pause appears. The moment between what happened and how I respond. **This is Andrum: the air between trigger and action.** Here the capacity to choose returns. Nothing is forced or fixed. It is simply seen and held. | **LEVEL 3**
**ALIGN**
*Act from a new place — dignity*

From this pause, a woman moves differently. Not from fear of loss, but from contact with herself. This is not an ideal to reach. It is a state that becomes available after the first two levels have been walked. **Over time, it becomes a reflex.** |
| --- | --- | --- |

Anchor · Allow · Align is both a framework for each practice and a map for the longer journey. The same three movements that happen in a 7-minute SOS practice also describe what changes over months of regular use.

### Kintsugi as the thread through all three levels

The Japanese art of repairing cracks with gold runs through every level as a unifying metaphor — not as a separate concept, but as the logic that holds the whole together.

> ◆ Anchor: the physiological reaction is not a malfunction — it is a signal. Anxiety is not the enemy. It is part of the system.
◆ Allow: the pause is the place where the crack is filled. Not hidden. Made visible, held with care.
◆ Align: the gold is what shows through when a woman chooses from herself rather than from fear.
> 

In the visual language, this is expressed literally: fine gold ornaments over photographs at the places of breath — chest, spine, solar plexus — as a reminder that inner dignity is present through every state, including the hardest ones.

## 02 · Brand positioning

The overall brand positioning covers all three levels and serves as the foundation for individual messages to each audience segment.

### Step 01 — Who she is

#### Who · In what moment

A woman with anxious attachment — in the moment when something in a relationship has set off a familiar wave: her body is already responding, her mind is trying to catch up.

On the surface she is composed — working, functioning, managing. But inside close relationships, a different version of herself switches on: one who feels every shift in a partner acutely, loses ground in uncertainty, and cannot stop the response through willpower alone.

She is self-aware — she has read about attachment, probably seen a therapist — and she still finds herself back inside the same cycle. She is not broken. Her nervous system learned to survive through hypervigilance.

### Step 02 — The core pain

#### The one thing they will pay to solve

**The body reacts before she can think — and she cannot stop it on her own in the moment it hits.**

Not abstract “anxious attachment,” but physically: a pounding heart, heat, tightness in the chest, a freeze, or the impossibility of sitting still.

She understands what is happening in her mind. But understanding does not stop the reaction. Therapy gave her awareness — it did not give her a tool for the moment. And it is precisely in that moment that she is alone.

Calling a friend for the third time feels like too much. Whatever she tried before does not work when it really hits.

### Step 03 — The transformation

#### From · To

From *“my body reacts and I can’t do anything”* → through *“I found the pause”* → to *“I act from myself, not from fear.”*

Not “heal from anxious attachment.” She knows that is not realistic in a short time. Instead: learn to walk through all three levels.

- **Anchor:** settle the body
- **Allow:** find the space between trigger and reaction
- **Align:** make a choice from contact with herself

Gradually, the path gets shorter. Over time, it becomes a reflex.

### Step 04 — The method

#### What makes this different

Breathwork practices created specifically for states of anxious attachment — working through the body, not trying to quiet the mind.

> ◆ Entry through physiology: the nervous system response is addressed before any reflection is possible
◆ Three formats for three levels: SOS (acute) — daily (support) — deep (integration)
◆ Not a universal meditation “for everyone” — a space that knows this specific experience
◆ A voice that does not rescue and does not teach — simply present while she finds her way back
>

## 03 · Positioning by segment

Two primary segments enter Andrum through different pain points. The three-level framework is the same for both — but the first message, the language, and the promise are different.

### Segment 1 · “In the wave”

*Internal name: RIGHT NOW · Product-facing: When it hits · 50–60% of traffic*

- Age 25–38
- In a relationship or recently out
- Every day is a rollercoaster

**Core pain:** the body is out of control and she cannot stop it right now

**Time horizon:** the next hour. No resource to choose. She needs one button.

**Self-perception:** “something is wrong with me.” Need: emergency help, not transformation.

#### Positioning for Segment 1

**When it hits and the body won’t listen —**

*Andrum gives her the first thing she needs: Anchor. Not explanations. Not a programme. Just a voice that leads her through breath back to the place where she can breathe again.*

*Entry language:* “hit me,” “can’t stop it,” “right now,” “give me my body back,” “no energy to choose.”

*At entry:* Level 1 — Anchor only. Allow and Align unfold through the practice. Philosophy comes after, never before.

<aside>
⛔

**What repels this segment**

Any concept on entry. Words about “inner kingdom” or “unfolding.” Transformation promises. Needing to read or choose anything before the practice starts.

</aside>

---

### Segment 2 · “On the shore”

*Internal name: LONG-TERM · Product-facing: Building ground · 30–35% of traffic*

- Age 27–40
- Out of the acute phase
- Functioning, but fragile inside

**Core pain:** understands the pattern, but has no daily tool that works with the body

**Time horizon:** 3–12 months. Has the capacity to choose. Looking for a system.

**Self-perception:** “I’m working on myself.” Need: a bridge between insight and daily practice.

#### Positioning for Segment 2

**When the understanding is there, but the ground still isn’t —**

*Andrum offers the full Anchor · Allow · Align path: daily practices that take her from settling the body, through finding the pause, to choosing from a new place. Until it becomes not an effort but a reflex.*

*Entry language:* “tired of cycles,” “want ground from inside,” “through the body not the mind,” “daily ritual,” “step by step,” “not just understanding — a practice.”

*All three levels are available and named from entry. She is ready for the full conversation about space and dignity.*

<aside>
✅

**What matters for this segment**

Honesty without idealization (not “21 days”). Balance of science and empathy. Real stories. Flexible structure without rigid scripts. Community as additional ground — “I am not alone in this.”

</aside>

## 04 · Archetype & brand narrative

### Inner ground — not a crown, but a state

The archetype of inner dignity is the third level of the concept, not the first. It is not named directly in communication.

A woman does not arrive looking for a “queen.” She arrives for her body. She discovers dignity herself after walking through the first two levels.

Psychologically, this is accurate: anxious attachment is life from a position where self-worth depends on another person’s reaction. A breathwork practice physically interrupts that cycle. Through the body, she returns to herself as her own center — not as an ideal, but as the only real point of ground.

> *Anxiety leads her away from herself. The body leads her back. The space between — that is where choice lives.*
> 

### The body as the path

In this concept, the body is not just a tool. It is the first language of self-knowledge. Breath works where words cannot: when the prefrontal cortex is offline, the nervous system still hears the rhythm of the inhale and exhale.

> ◆ The first inhale in a practice — the beginning of the return
◆ The pause between inhale and exhale — the physical experience of Andrum
◆ The exhale — a physiological act of release, not a metaphor
◆ Regular practice — a literal rewiring of the nervous system over time
> 

This is not “listen to your body” in a spiritual sense. It is physiology: the vagus nerve, heart rate variability, parasympathetic activation. The balance of science and empathy that the research audience asked for directly.

### Kintsugi — a thread through all three levels

Anxious episodes are not hidden and not “worked through to the end.” They become part of the story — cracks repaired with gold.

This is an honest metaphor for a nonlinear process: a woman can return to the wave even after months of practice. That is not a setback. It is the next seam.

> *Her cracks do not disappear. They become gold — visible, recognised, part of her value.*
> 

### Brand narrative in third person

She came from a place where her worth depended on someone else. Each time he pulled away, her body responded before her thoughts could. Not because she was weak. Her nervous system had simply learned to survive through hypervigilance.

The first thing she learned was not to stop the thoughts. It was to stop with the breath. To feel the floor under her feet. To return to her body before she sent the message she would regret. That was the first pause — a small Andrum.

Then there were more pauses. Then she started to notice the trigger before the fall. Then she began to act from a different place. Not because she was “fixed.” Because she found the way back to herself through her body — again and again, until it became a reflex.

Her cracks went nowhere. They simply became gold.

## 05 · Visual language & design

### Visual concept

The visual language is built around the same three levels. Every design choice reflects a layer of meaning. Anchor, Allow, and Align should be legible without words — through image, colour, composition, and the presence of space itself.

### Colour palette — two axes

The palette is built on two axes that reflect the duality of the concept: the warmth of the body and the cool of the air. Wholeness holds both — not only softness but strength, not only warmth but space.

*Warm axis: body, earth, depth, gold — Anchor and Align. Cool axis: air, space, pause — Allow. A single frame can hold both: that is an honest image of wholeness.*

*Avoid: acid accents, sterile white, cold meditative blues (#4A90D9 and similar), darkness without warmth.*

### Photography — three states

Photography is the primary carrier of meaning. Alive, warm, real. Not stock smiles. The body as the protagonist: skin, hair, hands, the chest, the line of the spine.

- **Anchor — Body in reaction:** a woman in a moment of tension. Recognisable state. Deeper shadow, more saturated warm tones. Needed for recognition: “this is about me.”
- **Allow — The space:** the moment of pause. Eyes closed. Breath steady. A lot of space in the frame. Transitional tones — warmth and coolness together.
- **Align — From ground:** open chest, calm gaze. Quiet dignity — not happiness on display. Lighter frames. More air. Cooler air tones alongside warm skin.

### Graphic elements

#### Gold ornaments — kintsugi as visual language

Fine gold lines overlaid on photographs at the places of breath and body: chest, solar plexus, along the spine.

> ○ Never over the face — only the body
○ Appear as a quiet anchor, never as the dominant element
○ Can appear across any of the three levels — gold is always present
> 

#### Air and space in composition

A lot of unfilled space in every frame — not emptiness, but meaning. The space around a woman is her inner territory. In anxiety it contracts. In ground it expands. Pauses between elements matter as much as the elements themselves.

> ○ One dominant image, minimal surrounding detail
○ Typefaces with air between letters, light weights
○ Blue and mist tones for “Allow” frames — the space between
> 

#### Collage techniques

- **Technique 1 — Two versions in one frame:** the primary figure in tension + a translucent version of herself in stillness (behind or beside her). Not the goal — the memory.
- **Technique 2 — Gold on the body:** a photo in practice (eyes closed, breath steady) with a gold ornament on the chest or along the spine.
- **Technique 3 — Gradient of states:** split frame from dark-saturated (Anchor) through transitional (Allow) to light-open (Align).
