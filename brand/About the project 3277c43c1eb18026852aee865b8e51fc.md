# About the project

## **ANDRUM**

*space to breathe*

*About the project, the philosophy, and the people behind it.*

### **What is Andrum?**

Andrum is a gentle space where you find tools to return to yourself in difficult emotional states.

Through the body — through breath — you come back to the place where you can feel the ground again, and where choice becomes possible.

Andrum is a Swedish word meaning *“space to breathe.”* Not as a metaphor — as a precise description of what the practice creates: a space where an automatic reaction used to be.

- The space between inhale and exhale — a physiological pause.
- The space between trigger and reaction — a moment where choice becomes possible.
- The space of inner ground — the place from which a woman moves differently.

We created Andrum as a quiet kind of support — a place you can come back to when life feels loud inside. A gentle reminder that you don’t have to move through the hard moments alone.

---

## **The philosophy: Anchor · Allow · Align**

At the heart of Andrum is a simple philosophy: we return to the body first, we make space for what is real, and we choose from a place of inner ground.

| **ANCHOR**
*Come back to the body* | **ALLOW**
*Open the space* | **ALIGN**
*Act from a new place* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We start with the body. We slow down enough to feel the ground again — feet, breath, skin, the present moment. Not to “fix” anything. Just to return. | We make space for what is real — panic, longing, shame, anger, numbness. Nothing is pushed away. Nothing is rushed. The feeling is allowed to move through, instead of being resisted. | From that space, a different choice becomes possible. Not a perfect version of you — just a truer one. A response that comes from connection with yourself, not from fear. |

*In Japanese kintsugi, broken pottery is repaired with gold. The crack is not hidden — it becomes part of the beauty. In Andrum, we hold our “cracks” the same way: not as flaws to erase, but as places that can be met with care — and slowly turned into value.*

## **The first collection: SOS Practices**

### **For when the wave hits — and you need the pause back**

A collection of **7 guided breathwork audios**, **5–10 minutes** each.

This kit was created for acute moments of anxiety that can arise in close connection — in a relationship, after a breakup, while dating, or even when you’re not in a relationship but your nervous system still carries the pattern.

It is for the moment when a message (or its silence) sets off the wave. When your chest tightens, your breath goes shallow, and the body reacts before the mind can catch up.

### **This is for her**

On the surface she is composed — working, functioning, managing. But inside close relationships, a different version of herself switches on: one who feels every shift in a partner acutely, loses ground in uncertainty, and cannot stop the response through willpower alone.

She is self-aware. She has read about attachment, has likely tried therapy or other forms of inner work — and still finds herself inside the same cycle. Not because she is broken, but because her nervous system learned hypervigilance as a way to survive.

She is looking for something that works right now. Not in three months. In the moment her hand is reaching for the phone, and her body is already reacting.

*She might recognise herself in this:*

- The body reacts before she can think — racing heart, heat, tightness, freeze
- She understands what is happening, but understanding does not stop the reaction
- She has tried to calm herself through thought — and it does not work when it really hits
- Calling a friend for the third time feels like too much
- She wants a tool that is always with her, that asks nothing of her except to breathe

### **What you get**

- **7 SOS practices** mapped to specific states (message anxiety, looping thoughts, shame, jealousy, urge to reach out, freeze/numbness, anger)
- **5–10 minute** sessions you can do anywhere
- A tool that is always within reach — even when a friend is unavailable, or you can’t speak to a therapist in that moment

### **What changes — and when**

We don’t promise transformation in 21 days. What the practices offer is a path — walked at your own pace, in your own time.

| **In the moment** | The body settles. The wave does not last as long. You do not send the message you would have regretted. You find the pause. |
| --- | --- |
| **After weeks** | The path back to yourself gets shorter. You recognise the trigger earlier — sometimes before the body has fully activated. You begin to trust that you can return. |
| **After months** | The reflex changes. What used to feel like an emergency begins to feel like information. Anxious attachment does not disappear — but you no longer live inside it all the time. |

Change is not linear. A woman can return to the wave even after months of practice. That is not a setback — it is the next layer of the same work. Each time, the path back is a little more familiar.

## **Who we are**

Andrum was created by three women who arrived here from different directions — breathwork, design, and building things for people to lean on.

We didn’t start with a concept. We started with the moment: the message that changes everything, the silence that feels like danger, the wave that rises before you can think. We know that wave — not from textbooks, but from our own bodies.

Before shaping this collection, we spent time in honest conversations with women who live inside the same pattern. We listened closely to what the acute moments actually feel like, what has helped, what hasn’t, and what has been missing.

We are not therapists. We are not here to “fix” you. We’re here to offer a steady tool for the moments when you’re alone with the wave — until the breath returns, the ground returns, and you can choose again.
